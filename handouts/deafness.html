Deafness in Dogs and Cats

Types of deafness

Congenital (present at birth) — common in pets with white coats or certain color patterns (e.g., merle, piebald). Hearing cells in the inner ear stop working shortly after birth. Usually permanent.

Acquired (develops later)

  • Sensorineural — damage to the inner-ear hair cells that turn sound into nerve signals. Causes: aging, illness or infection, injury, toxins or certain drugs. Usually permanent.
  • Conductive — sound cannot travel through the ear canal, eardrum or middle ear. Causes: ear infection, wax, fluid, polyps or tumors. Some forms are treatable.

Signs at home

  • Not responding to their name, doorbell or other sounds
  • Sleeping through loud noises
  • Startling when touched unexpectedly
  • Barking or meowing more (they cannot hear themselves)

Testing

BAER (brainstem auditory evoked response) — measures the brain's electrical response to sound. Painless, but pets must be still, so sedation may be needed.

  • Can tell: whether each ear hears or not, and at higher vs. lower sound levels
  • Cannot tell: how well your pet hears, which frequencies, the cause, or whether hearing will return

MRI or CT of the head can look for something blocking sound or a problem in the ear:

  • MRI — better for brain, nerves and soft tissue; recommended if there are other neurological signs (e.g., balance problems)
  • CT — better for bone and detailed ear structures

Living with a deaf pet

Safety

  • Leash or fenced area outdoors at all times
  • ID tag or microchip noting your pet is deaf; consider a locating (GPS) device
  • Avoid startling — approach where they can see you, or let them feel footsteps

Communication and training

  • Hand signals and visual cues; reward with treats (positive reinforcement)
  • Stomp on the floor (vibration) or flick a light to get attention
  • Use a gentle, consistent touch as a signal
  • Consider a trainer experienced with deaf pets

Quality of life

Deaf dogs and cats can live normal, happy lives. They often rely more on sight and smell. Deafness does not affect intelligence or their ability to be loving, well-behaved companions.
