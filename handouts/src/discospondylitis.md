## What is it?
Discospondylitis is an **infection of the disc** between two vertebrae and the bone around it. Bacteria or fungi usually travel through the bloodstream from elsewhere — the urinary tract, skin, heart valves or mouth. Untreated, it can spread to the spinal cord's covering (meningitis) or weaken the spine.

## Signs
- **Spinal pain** — the main sign
- Fever, poor appetite, weight loss
- As it worsens: pus buildup, slipped vertebrae or fracture can press on the spinal cord, causing **weakness, wobbliness or paralysis**

## Diagnosis
- **Imaging:** X-rays may not show changes until signs have been present for **2–6 weeks**. **MRI** finds the disease earlier, before major bone damage.
- **Finding the infection:** urinalysis and urine culture, blood cultures, **Brucella** test and other infectious disease tests. Sometimes the source is never found.

> **Until the Brucella result is back, avoid contact with your pet's urine** (wear gloves, wash hands). Brucella can infect people.

## Treatment
- **Antibiotics** (bacterial) or **antifungals** (fungal), chosen based on test results
- **Pain relief** — anti-inflammatories (usually NSAIDs)
- **Keep giving medication even when your pet seems well.** Bacterial infections need antibiotics for **at least 6 months**.
- **STRICT REST** — the infected disc can make the spine unstable and lead to a fracture. No running, jumping or rough play.

## Monitoring
- Follow-up **MRI or CT in 3–4 months**, or spinal X-rays every 6–8 weeks
- **C-reactive protein (CRP)** blood test every 3–4 months — it can detect a relapse before signs return

## Outlook
Most dogs respond quickly to pain medication and antibiotics. Prognosis depends on severity (pain only vs. unable to walk), type of infection and response to treatment. **Fungal** infections may need lifelong medication and carry a more guarded outlook. Some cases do not respond, leading to uncontrolled pain and declining quality of life.

## Call us if
Pain returns, fever, weakness or trouble walking, or your pet stops eating.
