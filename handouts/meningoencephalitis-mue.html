Meningoencephalitis (MUE)

What is it?

Meningoencephalitis means inflammation of the brain (encephal-) and its protective covering (meninges). When the spinal cord is involved it is called meningomyelitis. Inflammation can be caused by:

  • Infection (virus, bacteria, fungus or protozoa), or
  • An auto-immune reaction — the immune system overreacts and attacks the brain. This is called MUE (meningoencephalitis of unknown etiology).

Signs

Signs depend on which part of the brain is inflamed, and several areas can be affected at once.

  • Front of the brain: seizures, confusion, drowsiness, behavior change, pain
  • Back of the brain: poor balance, weakness, abnormal eye movements, pain

Diagnosis

  • MRI of the brain (or spine) to find inflammation and rule out tumors or fluid buildup
  • Spinal tap to analyze spinal fluid
  • Testing for common infectious diseases

Steroids given before testing (prednisone, prednisolone, dexamethasone) can make spinal fluid results less accurate.

Treatment

  • Infectious: antibiotics or antifungal drugs.
  • Auto-immune (MUE): medications that calm the immune system. We start with a higher steroid dose to "put out the fire." Because long-term high-dose steroids cause side effects, we usually add a second (sometimes third) immunosuppressive drug to keep the disease controlled while slowly weaning the steroid.

What to expect — "the rule of thirds"

For auto-immune meningoencephalitis:

  • About ⅓ do not improve and worsen within days
  • About ⅓ improve but have relapses
  • About ⅓ recover well with ongoing medication and care

Vaccinations

  • Do not vaccinate while your pet is being treated for auto-immune ME.
  • Vaccines may trigger a relapse, which can be more serious and harder to treat than the first episode. We recommend only the most necessary vaccines, decided case by case.
  • Antibody titer blood tests can show whether your pet is still protected against distemper, parvovirus and adenovirus. Titers cannot replace the legally required rabies vaccine.
  • Indoor pets have lower infection risk than active, outdoor pets. We cannot predict whether a vaccine will cause a relapse — there are risks either way, and we will help you decide.

Call us if

Seizures, worsening balance, new weakness, not eating, or possible medication side effects.
