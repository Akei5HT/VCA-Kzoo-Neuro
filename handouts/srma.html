Steroid-Responsive Meningitis-Arteritis (SRMA)

What is SRMA?

The meninges are protective layers around the brain and spinal cord. They contain blood vessels and spinal fluid. Meningitis means white blood cells have gathered in these layers — either to fight an infection or because the immune system attacks the body by mistake.

SRMA is a common immune-mediated meningitis in young dogs (typically 6–18 months), especially Beagles, Boxers, Bernese Mountain Dogs and Retrievers. We know it is immune-related because levels of an antibody (IgA) are high in blood and spinal fluid, and dogs improve quickly with steroids.

Signs

  • Severe neck pain — stiff neck, head held low, reluctance to move, crying out
  • Fever
  • Lethargy, poor appetite
  • Signs often come and go

Diagnosis

  • Spinal tap — spinal fluid shows high white blood cells (neutrophils)
  • Tests to make sure there is no infection
  • MRI or X-rays to rule out other problems
  • C-reactive protein (CRP) blood test — supports the diagnosis and tracks response to treatment

Treatment

  • Prednisone (a steroid) to calm the immune system
  • The dose is slowly reduced over several months once signs improve
  • We watch for steroid side effects (see our Corticosteroids handout)
  • If signs return, another immunosuppressive drug (such as cyclosporine) may be added
  • Rechecks with exams and CRP (and sometimes repeat spinal taps) guide the taper

Never stop prednisone suddenly — follow the tapering schedule exactly.

What to expect

The outlook is very good:

  • About 98% of dogs go into remission with treatment
  • About half are completely cured
  • 16–34% relapse at some point, but relapses usually respond to treatment again

Call us if

Neck pain, fever or lethargy return — especially as the dose is lowered.
