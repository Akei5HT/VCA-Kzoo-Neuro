---
title: Checking Your Pet's Vitals at Home
category: Home Care
---
Knowing how to check your pet's vital signs helps you **communicate with your veterinary team** and helps us figure out the cause of **"seizure-like" episodes** (for example, fainting from a heart problem).

| Vital sign | Normal (dog, at rest) | Concerning |
|---|---|---|
| Heart rate | 60–100 bpm (up to 120–140 when excited) | Below 50, above 160, or irregular |
| Breathing rate | 10–30 breaths/min | Labored, or above 40 at rest |
| Temperature | 100–102.5 °F | Below 99 or above 103 °F |
| Gum color | Pale pink, moist | Pale/white, bright red, blue/purple, yellow |

Small dogs and cats normally have faster heart rates (cats ~140–220 bpm).

## Heart rate
Feel the **femoral pulse** on the inside of the back leg, between the groin and the knee. Count beats for **15 seconds × 4** = beats per minute. Note whether the rhythm is **regular or irregular**.

## Breathing rate
Watch the chest rise and fall while your pet is resting or asleep. Count for **30 seconds × 2** = breaths per minute. Panting during activity is normal and is not the same as labored breathing.

## Body temperature
- **Rectal (most accurate):** use a digital thermometer with a disposable cover and lubricant. Gently insert into the rectum and wait for the reading. Stop if your pet becomes stressed or it is unsafe for you or your pet.
- **Armpit (less accurate):** with your pet sitting or standing, place the tip deep in the armpit and hold the leg down against the body until the reading is complete.

## Gum color
Gently lift the lip and look at the gums:
- **Pale or white** — shock, anemia
- **Bright red** — overheating, toxins
- **Blue or purple** — lack of oxygen, low body temperature, heart problems
- **Yellow** — liver disease, red blood cell destruction

**Take your pet to an emergency room right away for any life-threatening situation** — trouble breathing, collapse, blue or white gums, or a seizure longer than 5 minutes.
