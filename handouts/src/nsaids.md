## What are NSAIDs?
NSAIDs (pronounced "en-seds") are common pain-relieving and anti-inflammatory medications. Aspirin was the first NSAID — Native Americans used willow bark, a natural source, for pain relief. Human NSAIDs include ibuprofen and celecoxib (Celebrex). There are **NSAIDs made specifically for dogs** that manage pain safely and effectively, with no risk of addiction.

**Never give human NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen, aspirin) to your pet unless directed** — they can be toxic, especially to cats.

## Possible side effects
NSAIDs are generally safe, but can sometimes cause:
- **Stomach and intestinal upset or ulcers**
- **Kidney problems**
- Less commonly, liver or bone marrow problems

Give with food, and we may recommend periodic blood tests for long-term use.

## Stop the NSAID and call your veterinarian if your pet has
- Decreased appetite or vomiting
- **Dark, black or bloody diarrhea**
- Increased drinking or urination
- Lethargy, or **yellow gums, skin or eyes** (jaundice)
- Bruising or bleeding under the skin

## Never combine
**Do not give two NSAIDs together, and never give an NSAID with a steroid.** Together they can cause dangerous stomach and intestinal bleeding. If switching between them, ask us how long to wait.

| Common steroids | Common NSAIDs |
|---|---|
| Prednisone | Aspirin |
| Prednisolone | Carprofen (Rimadyl) |
| Dexamethasone | Etodolac (EtoGesic) |
| Methylprednisolone (Medrol) | Deracoxib (Deramaxx) |
| Triamcinolone | Meloxicam (Metacam) |
| Cortisone | Firocoxib (Previcox) |
| Budesonide | Grapiprant (Galliprant), Robenacoxib (Onsior) |
