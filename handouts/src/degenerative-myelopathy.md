## What is it?
Degenerative myelopathy is a slowly progressive disease of the spinal cord, similar to ALS (Lou Gehrig's disease) in people. It begins in the mid-back portion of the spinal cord and spreads. The exact cause is unknown, but it is linked to a mutation in the **SOD1 gene**. **DM is not painful.**

## Which dogs get DM?
Older medium to large breed dogs, usually over 8 years (average 9). Highest risk: **German Shepherds** and **Boxers**. Also: Pembroke and Cardigan Welsh Corgis, Siberian Husky, Poodles, Chesapeake Bay Retriever, Rhodesian Ridgeback, Bernese Mountain Dog, Golden Retriever, Kerry Blue and Soft-Coated Wheaten Terriers, Wire Fox Terrier, American Eskimo Dog and Pug.

## How it progresses
| Stage | Timing (approx.) | Signs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | First 6–12 months | Wobbly, weak back legs, often one side worse; "walking funny" |
| 2 | Next 6–12 months | Muscle loss, unable to move back legs or tail; leaking urine/stool |
| 3 | 14–24 months | Front legs become weak |
| 4 | After ~36 months | All four legs paralyzed; trouble breathing and swallowing |

## Testing
A **DNA test** (cheek swab via OFA, ofa.org, or blood via University of Missouri) gives results in 1–4 weeks:
- **Normal** — no mutation
- **Carrier** — one copy; rarely develops DM
- **Affected / At risk** — two copies; may develop DM, but not all do and we cannot predict when

**The DNA test alone cannot diagnose DM.** Diagnosis is based on breed, age, signs and ruling out other spinal problems (such as disc disease) with **MRI** and spinal fluid testing. A definitive diagnosis is only possible by examining the spinal cord after death. Dogs testing Normal or Carrier that show signs need other causes investigated.

## Treatment
There is **no cure**. What helps:
- **Daily gentle exercise** — maintains strength and may prolong mobility
- Physical rehabilitation; laser therapy and acupuncture may help
- No supplement has been proven to work
- Wheelchairs (carts), harnesses and good nursing care as mobility declines — see our *Nursing Care for Limited Mobility* handout

## Prognosis
DM is progressive. Without support, most dogs lose use of the back legs within **6–12 months** of diagnosis. When the **front legs weaken**, humane euthanasia is often considered; we will help you assess quality of life.
