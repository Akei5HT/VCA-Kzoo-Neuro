Trigeminal Nerve Dysfunction ("Dropped Jaw")

Key point: most dogs with a dropped jaw on both sides have idiopathic trigeminal neuritis and recover within 2–3 weeks with supportive care.

What is it?

The trigeminal nerve (cranial nerve V) controls the chewing muscles and provides feeling to the face. When it does not work, the dog cannot close its mouth and has trouble eating.

Causes

  • Idiopathic trigeminal neuritis (ITN) — about 53% of cases. Inflammation of unknown cause, usually both sides, usually resolves on its own.
  • Inflammatory neuritis — about 16%; immune-related, one or both sides.
  • Tumors — about 31%; affect one side only, more common in older dogs.

Both sides affected → about a 96% chance it is ITN. One side affected → imaging is needed to look for a tumor.

Signs

  • Dropped jaw — cannot close the mouth
  • Food and water falling from the mouth; drooling
  • Over time, muscle wasting of the temples and cheeks
  • Less often: loss of facial feeling, Horner's syndrome (small pupil, droopy eyelid)

Most dogs with ITN have normal facial feeling and remain bright and comfortable.

Diagnosis

  • Neurological exam; ruling out jaw injury, rabies exposure and masticatory muscle myositis
  • MRI — best test for tumors or nerve inflammation, especially if one-sided or atypical
  • Blood tests (including thyroid); sometimes spinal fluid analysis

Treatment

  • ITN: self-limiting. Steroids are sometimes used early, but there is little evidence they speed recovery.
  • Inflammatory neuritis: may respond to immunosuppressive medication.
  • Tumors: stereotactic radiation therapy or surgery in select cases.

Care at home — the most important part

  • Hand-feed meatballs of soft canned food, placed at the back of the tongue; your dog can still swallow
  • Raise food and water bowls; offer gruel/slurry of canned food and water
  • Water by syringe (no needle) in the corner of the mouth — squirt slowly and let your dog swallow
  • Keep your dog hydrated — check gum moisture
  • Wipe the mouth and face after meals to prevent skin irritation
  • Weigh weekly — major weight loss may mean a temporary feeding tube is needed

Expected recovery (ITN)

  • Weeks 1–2: still cannot close the jaw; intensive hand-feeding
  • Weeks 2–3: gradual improvement
  • Weeks 3–4: most dogs are eating normally. Recurrence is possible but uncommon.

Call us if

Your dog is not drinking or seems dehydrated, loses more than 10% of body weight, has breathing trouble, is not improving after 3 weeks, or shows new signs (head tilt, circling, seizures, weakness).
