What to Expect for a Spinal Tap

Why do we do a spinal tap?

  • To check for inflammation or infection of the brain or spinal cord
  • Usually done right after an MRI to rule out other problems

An MRI gives us pictures, but it cannot give us a sample. Taking a tissue sample from the brain or spinal cord is difficult and risky. A spinal tap is the closest way to sample the nervous system without surgery.

The procedure

Your pet is under general anesthesia. A small area is shaved and cleaned, and a needle is used to collect a small amount of spinal fluid (cerebrospinal fluid, CSF) from one of two places:

  • High tap (cerebellomedullary cistern) — at the base of the skull
  • Low tap (lumbar cistern) — in the lower back

Your neurologist chooses the safest, most useful site for your pet's condition.

What happens to the fluid?

It is sent to a laboratory for:

  • Fluid analysis — protein level and cell examination
  • Infectious disease testing (viral, fungal, protozoal) when needed
  • Special tests if warranted, such as PARR for lymphoma

After the spinal tap

  • Unlike people, pets rarely get headaches or other side effects.
  • Serious complications are rare (less than 1%).
  • Most pets eat soon after the procedure.
  • The shaved area may look a little red or bruised for a few days.

Contact us if your pet stops eating, seems painful, or shows new weakness or other neurological signs.
