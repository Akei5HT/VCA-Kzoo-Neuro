Seizure-Like Episodes

Not every "episode" is a seizure. Several conditions can look very similar, and telling them apart changes the treatment.

Conditions that can look like seizures

  • Vestibular (balance) episodes — sudden head tilt, rolling, abnormal eye movements
  • Fainting (syncope) — brief collapse from a heart rhythm or blood pressure problem
  • Metabolic disease — low blood sugar, electrolyte imbalances
  • Neuromuscular disease — episodic weakness or collapse
  • Movement disorders — abnormal movements while fully awake
  • REM sleep behavior disorder — acting out dreams during sleep
  • Neck pain — sudden crying, trembling or stiffening
  • Spikes in pressure inside the skull
  • Cataplexy — sudden loss of muscle tone, often with excitement
  • Panic or anxiety attacks
  • Myoclonus — sudden, brief muscle jerks

How you can help us tell the difference

  • Video the episode whenever it is safe — this is the single most helpful thing you can do.
  • Note whether your pet was aware and responsive during the episode.
  • Note what happened before (sleeping, exercise, excitement, eating) and after (confused, tired, or normal right away).
  • If you can, check heart rate, breathing and gum color (see our Checking Vitals at Home handout).
  • Keep a log of the date, time and length of each episode.

Tests that may help

  • Neurological and physical exam, blood work
  • Heart evaluation if fainting is suspected
  • MRI and spinal fluid analysis
  • EEG (electroencephalogram) — records the brain's electrical activity and is very useful for telling true seizures from seizure-like episodes

If an episode lasts longer than 5 minutes, or your pet does not return to normal afterward, seek emergency care.
