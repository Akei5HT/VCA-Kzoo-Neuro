Seizures — Frequently Asked Questions

What is a seizure?

A seizure is a sudden burst of abnormal electrical activity in the brain. It causes abnormal muscle movement or stiffening and may change awareness. A seizure is a sign, not a disease — it tells us some brain cells are misbehaving.

What causes seizures?

  • Outside the brain: toxins, some drugs, low blood sugar, liver, kidney or other metabolic disease
  • Inside the brain: tumor, inflammation (meningoencephalitis), stroke, bleeding, trauma or old scar tissue
  • Idiopathic epilepsy: most likely in otherwise healthy dogs 1–6 years old — they are born with a lower seizure threshold
  • Pets under 1 year → birth defects, infection, metabolic disease are more likely. Older pets → brain tumor or stroke

How do we find the cause?

A neurological exam and detailed history (to confirm the events are seizures), blood work, brain MRI and spinal fluid analysis. Finding the cause leads to better control.

Is my flea/tick or heartworm product the cause?

Isoxazoline products (Bravecto, Credelio, NexGard, Simparica) carry an FDA seizure warning, but reactions are rare and usually happen within 24 hours of a dose. Heartworm preventives at normal doses are considered safe, even in dogs with the MDR1 mutation.

When do we start medication?

  • More than 1 seizure every 6 weeks
  • A seizure lasting 3 minutes or longer
  • More than 1 seizure in 24 hours (cluster)
  • Slow or severe recovery after a seizure
  • A known progressive cause (tumor, inflammation)

"Seizures beget seizures" — each seizure makes the next one easier, and untreated seizures can recruit healthy brain cells and become harder to control.

What should I expect?

Seizures are managed, not cured. Our goal is no more than 1 seizure every 6–8 weeks with acceptable side effects. Most dogs are well controlled on 1–2 medications; about 15–30% remain difficult to control. Doses may be adjusted over weeks to months. After 12 months seizure-free we may discuss a slow taper. Australian Shepherds and Border Collies often have more severe epilepsy and do best with early, aggressive treatment.

Diet and supplements

These complement — never replace — seizure medication.

  • Purina NeuroCare diet or MCT oil (C8/C10 only, e.g., Whole Foods 365 brand). MCT oil: give twice daily with food; start with ½ tsp and increase by ½ tsp every 3 days. If diarrhea occurs, stop until stools are normal for 2 days, then restart at the last well-tolerated dose.
  • Home-cooked or ketogenic diets: work with a veterinary nutritionist (BalanceIT.com, petdiets.com).
  • CBD oil is not recommended as a stand-alone treatment and may change seizure-drug blood levels.
  • On potassium bromide? Avoid salty snacks and make any diet change gradually over 4–6 weeks.

Helping at home

  • Keep a seizure log: date, time, length, what it looked like and anything unusual that day. Bring it to every visit.
  • Keep a regular sleep schedule — sleep loss can trigger seizures.
  • During a seizure: keep your pet away from stairs and water, keep your hands away from the mouth, and time it.

Go to the ER if:

  • A seizure lasts longer than 5 minutes
  • More than 3 seizures in a day (or more than your pet's usual cluster)
  • Your pet does not return to normal between seizures

Death from seizures is very unlikely, except with prolonged seizures that do not respond to emergency treatment.
